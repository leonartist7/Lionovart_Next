## 0. Why it currently reads flat (honest diagnosis)

1. **One tone, one rhythm.** Everything is white, centered, evenly padded. No dark beat, no
   full-bleed moment, no scale jump. The eye never gets a jolt → reads as "calm template."
2. **Benefits disappear.** The phone section fades each line in and out, so the visitor never
   *accumulates* the case. Conversion needs **stacking** proof — each benefit should land and stay.
3. **Motion is decorative, not narrative.** Things move, but nothing *builds*. High-converting
   motion makes the user feel a case being assembled.
4. **Not enough humans / outcomes.** One face (Marc) helps; the page needs 1–2 more human/outcome
   anchors so it feels like a business with clients, not a portfolio shell.

**Design principles we'll apply** (the antidote):
- **Contrast rhythm** — alternate white ↔ one dark "cinema" beat so the page breathes.
- **Accumulate, don't flash** — benefits stack and persist; the list is the argument.
- **Video as proof** — real motion in every device.
- **Asymmetry + scale jumps** — big type next to small labels, off-center compositions.
- **One job per screen** — every section drives toward "book the call."

## 1. PHONE / MOBILE SECTION — three directions

All three: phone(s) hold **looping app video**, benefits **stack and stay**, sticky-scroll reveal.

### Proposal A — "The Spec Sheet" ⭐ recommended
**Concept:** one big phone (app video) pinned to the right; on the left, benefits reveal **one by
one and remain**, building a checklist connected by a thin red spine. By the end, the full list
stands beside the phone — a complete, scannable case.

**Layout:** 55/45 split. Left = stacked benefit rows (icon/number · bold benefit · one-line proof),
each joined by a vertical red line that "fills" as rows appear. Right = phone, slight tilt, soft
floor shadow, looping video. Section eyebrow top-left, micro-CTA bottom-left ("See it live →").

**Motion:** phone rises once → pins. Benefit rows slide up + fade in sequentially as you scroll and
**stay**; the red spine grows to connect them. Peak: all rows lit, phone glowing.

**Why it converts:** cumulative proof (Zeigarnik + commitment) — the visitor watches the case grow
and finishes with everything visible. Cleanest hierarchy, strongest on mobile (rows stack naturally).

> connected by a thin vertical red line running through them. Minimalist, editorial, high-end agency

### Proposal B — "Device Triptych"
**Concept:** three phones in a staggered row, each looping a **different** app screen (booking /
dashboard / checkout), each with a short caption beneath. Shows breadth of what you build for mobile.

**Layout:** 3 phones, center one largest and forward, outer two smaller + receding, gentle arc.
Captions under each. Headline above ("One build. Every screen they need.").

**Motion:** phones rise in a quick stagger (center first), then float micro-parallax; captions fade in
and stay. On scroll-out, they tilt toward the viewer slightly.

**Why it converts:** demonstrates range + concreteness (real app contexts) — answers "can you build
*my* kind of app?" Visually rich, gallery feel.

**Image prompt:**

### Proposal C — "Cinema Beat" (the dark break the page needs)
**Concept:** this section goes **full-bleed near-black** — the one cinematic contrast moment. A big
phone with app video glows center; benefit cards (frosted glass) **assemble and stack** to one side;
one card is a mini face + quote. Then it transitions back to white.

**Layout:** dark ground, phone center with a subtle red rim-glow, glass benefit cards stacking on the
right, eyebrow + headline upper-left. One card = small client photo + 6-word quote.

**Motion:** phone rises into a spotlight; glass cards fly in and **lock into a neat stack** (stay);
red underlines draw under each. Peak-end cinematic.

**Why it converts:** the tonal break makes the device + video *pop* (Von Restorff), adds the premium
"cinema" beat the all-white page is missing, and the face card injects trust at the moment of proof.

**Image prompt:**
> Cinematic dark charcoal (#0a0a0a) product section, a large smartphone mockup glowing in a center
> spotlight with a subtle red rim light, screen showing a premium app UI. To the right, a neat
> vertical stack of frosted-glass benefit cards with white text and thin red underline accents; one
> card shows a small smiling client portrait with a short quote. Dramatic studio lighting, deep
> shadows, premium high-end agency aesthetic, red #e5192a accent, sophisticated and expensive looking.
> 16:9.

## 2. PAGE-LEVEL ELEVATION (applies regardless of phone choice)

1. **Add ONE dark cinema beat** (Proposal C is a natural home, or a short proof band). Breaks the
   flat white, gives peak-end drama. *Why: rhythm = perceived production value.*
2. **Video in every device** — hero display, build-showcase center, phone(s), niche previews. *Why:
   motion reads as "real, working product," not mockup.*
3. **A testimonial strip** lower on the page: 4–5 small client faces + one-line outcomes, horizontal.
   *Why: consensus proof; "people like me hired them."* (You have the `/Testimonials` photos.)
4. **Sharper close.** The final CTA gets the biggest type + a scarcity/founder line, on dark, peak
   motion. *Why: peak-end rule — last impression = decisiveness.*
5. **Type contrast.** Push the display headlines bigger, labels smaller/tracked. *Why: hierarchy
   jumps feel designed, not defaulted.*
6. **Asymmetry pass.** Offset a few sections off-center (like the stats split). *Why: tension =
   "art-directed," the opposite of template.*
